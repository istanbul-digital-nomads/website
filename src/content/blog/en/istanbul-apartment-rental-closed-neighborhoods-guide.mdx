## The short version

Renting an apartment in Istanbul requires navigating Turkey's **closed neighborhood quotas (Kapalı Mahalleler)**, where foreign residence permits (*İkamet*) are permanently blocked across entire districts including Fatih, Esenyurt, and sections of Kadikoy and Sisli. In 2026, real estate agent fees are capped by law at **one month's rent + 20% VAT**, and typical move-in costs require roughly **3.2 to 4 months of upfront cash** (deposit, first month, agent fee, and notary filings).

For stays under 90 days, remote workers should use verified monthly rentals on Flatio or Airbnb. For stays of six months or longer requiring an İkamet, you must sign a notarized lease in an open neighborhood, verify the title deed (*Tapu*), and register building utilities under your name.

| **Monthly Building Fee** | 300 - 8,000 TL depending on building type | Clarify Aidat in writing before signing the contract |

## What is the "Closed Neighborhood" (Kapalı Mahalle) law?

To maintain demographic balance, Turkey's Presidency of Migration Management (*Göç İdaresi Başkanlığı*) enacted a strict rule: any neighborhood where the registered foreign population reaches **20% of the total census** is immediately closed to new foreign residence registrations.

Nationwide, over 1,169 neighborhoods are closed. In Istanbul, this policy impacts major parts of both the European and Asian sides:

- **Fully Closed Districts:** The entire district of **Fatih** (the historic peninsula, Sultanahmet, Balat, Fener) and the entire district of **Esenyurt** are completely closed to all new foreigner registrations.
- **Partially Closed Districts:** High-demand nomad zones have individual quarters shuttered. In **Beyoğlu**, neighborhoods like Kocatepe, Şehit Muhtar, and Bülbül are closed. In **Şişli**, central quarters including Mecidiyeköy, İnönü, and Ergenekon are blocked. In **Beşiktaş**, Sinanpaşa is closed. In **Kadıköy**, select sectors surrounding the central market and transport hubs face strict quotas.

> **The Costly Trap:** Unscrupulous or uninformed real estate agents (*emlakçı*) will frequently tell foreigners: *"Don't worry, the notary will stamp the lease, so you can apply."* **This is misleading.** A Turkish notary will notarize any valid contract between two adults—notaries do not check immigration quotas. Göç İdaresi will simply reject your residency file at your appointment, leaving you trapped in a 1-year non-refundable lease.

## How to find an apartment: The 3 main routes

### 1. Sahibinden.com (The Turkish Standard)

Sahibinden is the default classified platform across Turkey. Almost all rental inventory passes through it.

- **Filters to use:** Under *Kiralık Daire* (Apartment for Rent), filter by district (*İlçe*) and neighborhood (*Mahalle*). Check *Sahibinden* (direct from owner) to skip agent commissions, though owner listings make up less than 15% of active inventory.
- **Language barrier:** The platform is in Turkish. Use Chrome auto-translate or browse with a Turkish-speaking contact.
- **Tip:** When messaging agents on Sahibinden, write directly via WhatsApp in clear Turkish or English: specify your profession, nationality, planned move-in date, and ask explicitly if the neighborhood is open for foreign residence.

### 2. Specialized Medium-Term Platforms (Flatio & Blueground)

If you are staying for 1 to 6 months and do not want to set up Turkish utility contracts (water, gas, electricity, Wi-Fi):

- **Blueground:** Premium furnished apartments in upscale areas (Nişantaşı, Levent, Bomonti, Kadıköy). Very expensive ($1,600 - $3,500/month), but completely frictionless with English support and all utilities included.
- **Flatio:** Catered specifically to digital nomads with verified internet speed tests and flexible deposit protection.

### 3. Word of Mouth and Nomad Community Channels

Expats and remote workers moving out of Istanbul frequently hand over their furnished leases or recommend vetted landlords without agent fees. Explore active verified listings in the [Istanbul Nomads Listings Hub](/listings) and connect with members in our community circles.

## Moving costs breakdown: What cash do you need upfront?

Securing an unfurnished or furnished apartment in Istanbul requires substantial liquid cash on day one. Landlords and agents rarely accept credit cards; everything is transacted via Turkish Lira bank transfers.

Assuming a representative 1-bedroom (1+1) apartment in Kadikoy or Sisli renting for **30,000 TL/month** (~$750 USD):

| Expense Item | Calculation / Formula | Amount (TL) | Amount (USD approx.) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **First Month's Rent** | 1 month in advance | 30,000 TL | ~$750 |
| **Security Deposit (Depozito)** | 1 to 2 months rent (typically 1 for unfurnished, 2 for furnished) | 30,000 - 60,000 TL | ~$750 - $1,500 |
| **Agent Commission (Emlakçı)** | 1 month rent + 20% VAT (legal maximum) | 36,000 TL | ~$900 |
| **Notary & Sworn Translator** | Mandatory lease certification | ~2,500 - 4,000 TL | ~$65 - $100 |
| **Utility Deposits (Abonelikler)** | Gas (İGDAŞ), Water (İSKİ), Electricity (Enerjisa) | ~4,000 - 6,500 TL | ~$100 - $160 |
| **Total Move-in Capital** | **First month + deposit + fee + filings** | **102,500 - 136,500 TL** | **~$2,550 - $3,410** |

## Understanding Aidat: The hidden monthly maintenance fee

In Turkey, the monthly rent does not include **Aidat** (the building service charge).

- **Historic walkups (Kadikoy, Moda, Cihangir, Galata):** Typically 300 to 800 TL/month. Covers hallway lighting, weekly staircase cleaning, and municipal bin collection.
- **Modern Gated Complexes (Siteler in Bomonti, Maslak, Atasehir):** Typically 3,500 to 9,000+ TL/month. Covers 24/7 private security guards, high-speed elevators, underground parking, fitness centers, and central heating base tariffs.

> **Always ask before you sign:** *"Aylık aidat ne kadar?"* (How much is the monthly building fee?). Ensure the lease contract explicitly states that previous building debts incurred by the landlord or former tenants are not your responsibility.

## The 6 golden rules before signing a lease

1. **Verify the Title Deed (Tapu):** Ask the landlord or agent for a copy of the *Tapu*. Verify that the owner's full name on the title deed matches the identification and the bank IBAN you are wiring money to. Never wire rent to an agent's personal account.
2. **Include DASK Policy Number:** For residency registration and utility hookups, the apartment must have valid compulsory earthquake insurance (*DASK*). The 10-digit DASK policy number must be written directly into the lease.
3. **Beware the Eviction Notice (Tahliye Taahhütnamesi):** Landlords frequently demand that tenants sign an eviction undertaking. If signed without a date, landlords can fill in an arbitrary date later to force an early departure. **Never sign an undated eviction undertaking.**
4. **Wire Rent with a Clear Bank Description:** Under Turkish tax code, all residential rents exceeding 500 TL must pass through a regulated banking channel. In the wire transfer description, always write:  
   `[Month] [Year] Kira Bedeli - [Apartment Address]`
5. **Inspect Water Pressure & Fiber Infrastructure:** Before committing, test the water pressure in the shower and verify fiber optic terminal availability (TurkNet or Turkcell Superonline fiber box in the basement). See our [Internet and SIM Guide](/blog/turkey-phone-registration-imei-sim-guide) for connectivity standards.
6. **Double-Check Göç İdaresi Address Registration (UAVT Code):** Every building unit in Turkey has a unique 10-digit National Address Database code (*UAVT / NVİ Adres Kodu*). Request this code from the agent and check that the unit is registered as residential (*Mesken*), not commercial office (*İşyeri*).

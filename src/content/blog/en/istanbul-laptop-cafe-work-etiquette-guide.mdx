## The unwritten rules of cafe work in Istanbul

Turkish hospitality (*misafirperverlik*) is world-renowned, and baristas in Istanbul are remarkably warm and tolerant. However, the surge in remote workers since 2023 has caused several boutique cafes to introduce polite restrictions. Understanding these social boundaries ensures you will always be welcomed back.

### 1. The Weekend Laptop Curfew

If you walk into an artisan coffee shop in Moda (Kadıköy) or Cihangir (Beyoğlu) at 13:00 on a Saturday, you may see subtle signs on tables reading: *"Laptop-free zone on weekends"* or *"Hafta sonu bilgisayarsız saatler"*.

- **Why this exists:** Independent cafes rely on weekend brunch traffic (eggs, pastries, Turkish breakfast spreads) to survive high commercial rents and inflation. A laptop user nursing a single filter coffee for three hours prevents dozens of paying brunch guests from sitting down.
- **The nomad solution:** On weekends, either take the day off to explore the Prince Islands or head to massive work hubs like **Espressolab Roastery** (Merter or Caddebostan), **Federal Coffee**, or book a weekend day pass at **Kolektif House**.

### 2. The 90-Minute Re-Order Cadence

A polite rule of thumb in Istanbul is the **90 to 120-minute cycle**:
- **Hour 0:** Order your specialty pour-over or flat white.
- **Hour 2:** Order a fresh Turkish tea (*çay*), a sparkling mineral water (*maden suyu*), or a cookie.
- **Hour 4:** Order a lunch sandwich or wrap, or tip the barista generously when packing up.

Supporting independent cafes financially keeps Istanbul's independent coffee scene alive and maintains goodwill between nomads and locals.

### Navigating plugs and cables

- **Communal Work Tables:** Most work-friendly venues concentrate power outlets around central wooden communal tables or perimeter bar stools. Look for power strips mounted underneath the tabletops.
- **Safety First:** Never run your MacBook MagSafe cord across a narrow walking aisle where waiters or elderly patrons might trip. If a seat has no plug nearby, top up your battery before arriving or carry a 65W portable power bank.

## Taking video and voice calls politely

The acoustic environment in Turkish cafes varies wildly, from quiet classical jazz to loud Mediterranean chatter.

- **Acceptable:** Wearing noise-canceling headphones (AirPods Pro with Voice Isolation), muting your microphone when not speaking, and answering a 2-minute emergency question from your team.
- **Unacceptable:** Placing your laptop on full speaker volume, running an energetic 45-minute sales demo inside a quiet room, or shouting over the espresso grinder.
- **Where to go for calls:** Move to the outdoor garden patio, find a semi-enclosed balcony table, or walk to a nearby park bench overlooking the Bosphorus if the weather is clear.

## The short version

Turkey allows foreign mobile phones to connect to local cellular networks for **120 days per IMEI slot** before the Information and Communication Technologies Authority (BTK) blocks the device. In 2026, registering a foreign phone costs over **45,000 TL** in government taxes, making registration impractical for remote workers and nomads staying under one year.

Instead of paying the hefty tax, most digital nomads use the **dual-SIM trick** (120 days on physical SIM + 120 days on eSIM = 240 days), carry a **portable pocket Wi-Fi router**, or purchase an affordable **local secondary Android phone** to hotspot their primary device.

## What is Turkey's 120-day IMEI registration rule?

Under regulations enforced by Turkey's Information and Communication Technologies Authority (BTK), any mobile device imported from abroad that connects to Turkish cellular towers (Turkcell, Türk Telekom, or Vodafone) is granted a 120-day grace period. Once this 120-day counter expires, the device's International Mobile Equipment Identity (IMEI) number is blacklisted on Turkish networks.

A blacklisted phone is **not broken or locked permanently**. The restriction only applies to Turkish cellular voice, SMS, and cellular data connections. Your phone will continue to work perfectly on Wi-Fi networks in cafes, apartments, and coworking spaces, and will operate normally as soon as you leave Turkey.

```
Foreign Phone Arrives in Istanbul
               │
               ▼
Inserts Turkish SIM Card (Turkcell / Vodafone / Türk Telekom)
               │
               ▼
BTK Network Detection: 120-Day Timer Begins
               │
       ┌───────┴───────┐
       ▼               ▼
Day 1 to 120:     Day 120+:
Full Cellular     Cellular Blocked (Wi-Fi Still Works 100%)
Signal & 4G/5G    Device Unlocks on Next Jan 1st or Outside Turkey
```

### How the countdown is calculated

The 120-day countdown starts the moment your foreign phone's IMEI transmits to a Turkish cell tower using a local Turkish SIM card or active international roaming. 

If you visit Istanbul for two weeks in May and return for two weeks in September, the intervening months when your phone was abroad may still count toward the rolling window unless your line was disconnected. BTK sends warning SMS messages to your phone as the deadline approaches (usually at 90 days, 60 days, and 30 days remaining).

## How does the dual-SIM trick grant 240 days of legal access?

Modern smartphones (including iPhone XR and newer, Google Pixel 3 and newer, and Samsung Galaxy S20 and newer) feature **two separate IMEI numbers**: one for the physical nano-SIM card slot (IMEI 1) and one for the internal eSIM chip (IMEI 2).

Because BTK monitors and blocks individual IMEI numbers rather than phone hardware serial numbers, you can legally double your mobile lifespan in Turkey:

1. **Days 1 to 120:** Purchase a physical nano-SIM card from Turkcell or Türk Telekom and place it into your primary physical SIM tray (IMEI 1).
2. **Days 110 to 120:** When you receive the BTK warning SMS indicating your physical IMEI is about to expire, visit an operator store or online portal and request an **eSIM migration**.
3. **Days 121 to 240:** Activate the eSIM profile on your phone's secondary slot (IMEI 2). BTK registers this as a new, previously unseen IMEI, granting you a fresh 120-day window.

> **Important:** Always check your phone's settings (`Settings > General > About` on iOS, or dial `*#06#` on any device) to confirm that your phone displays two distinct 15-digit IMEI numbers before relying on this method.

## Does the 120-day counter reset on January 1st?

Yes. BTK's enforcement mechanism operates on a **calendar-year cycle**. 

On January 1st of every calendar year, the 120-day unregistered allowance resets for foreign IMEI numbers that were previously connected. If your foreign phone was blocked in November, its cellular capability will automatically restore on January 1st, giving you another 120 days for the new year.

For digital nomads arriving in autumn (e.g., September or October), this calendar reset is a massive benefit: your 120 days will carry you into December, and on January 1st you gain another full 120 days without taking any action.

## How much is the Turkish phone registration tax in 2026?

The official passenger phone registration tax (*Yolcu Beraberinde Getirilen Telefon Harcı*) has grown into one of the highest device import fees in the world. 

| Year | Registration Fee (TL) | Approximate USD Value | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **2023** | 6,091 TL → 20,000 TL | ~$800 | Mid-year emergency adjustment |
| **2024** | 31,692 TL | ~$980 | Revaluation rate adjustment |
| **2025** | ~38,500 TL | ~$1,050 | Annual fiscal indexation |
| **2026** | **45,614 TL** | **~$1,150+** | Current statutory tariff |

### The hidden strings attached to official registration

Paying the 45,000+ TL fee is not simply a matter of money. Turkish tax law enforces strict operational restrictions on registered phones:

1. **Passport Entry Requirement:** You must have entered Turkey on a foreign passport within the last 365 calendar days.
2. **One Phone Every Three Years:** A single passport holder can only register one phone every three calendar years.
3. **The Single-User SIM Lock:** The registered phone will **only function with a SIM card registered to the exact same Turkish Tax Identification Number (Vergi No) or Foreigner ID Number (Yabancı Kimlik No)** as the passport used for registration. You cannot lend the phone to a friend or partner, nor can you sell it on the Turkish second-hand market for 3 years.

## The 3 practical alternatives for remote workers

Given the exorbitant registration tax, seasoned remote workers and digital nomads in Istanbul use three proven alternatives:

### 1. The Portable 4G/5G Pocket Wi-Fi (MiFi)

A portable battery-powered LTE router (such as a TP-Link M7350, Netgear Nighthawk, or a local operator Vınn) costs between 1,500 TL and 3,500 TL ($40 - $90 USD). 

- You buy a local data SIM card and insert it into the pocket router.
- Your foreign smartphone and laptop connect to the pocket router via Wi-Fi.
- **The huge advantage:** Because your foreign phone never inserts a Turkish SIM, its cellular radio never connects to Turkish cell towers. **The 120-day countdown timer on your phone never even starts.**

### 2. The Local Secondary "Hotspot" Phone (Burner Setup)

Walk into any authorized technology retailer in Istanbul (MediaMarkt, Teknosa, or an official Xiaomi/Samsung store) and buy an entry-level smartphone with a Turkish IMEI (such as a Redmi Note or Samsung A-series) for 6,000 to 9,000 TL ($160 - $240 USD).

- Use the local phone for all Turkish mobile calls, SMS banking verifications, and cellular data.
- Turn on your portable Wi-Fi hotspot on the local phone to broadcast internet to your primary iPhone or MacBook.
- When your stay in Turkey ends, you can resell the phone or keep it for future visits.

### 3. Rely on Istanbul's Dense Wi-Fi Infrastructure

If your work is primarily desk-based, you can operate entirely without a local SIM. Istanbul has one of the densest fiber-connected cafe and coworking ecosystems in Southern Europe:

- Coworking spaces like Kolektif House, Workinton, and Impact Hub offer 100-500 Mbps redundant fiber connections.
- Specialty coffee chains like Espressolab, Kronotrop, and Petra Coffee provide fast Wi-Fi and ample power outlets across Kadikoy, Moda, Cihangir, and Besiktas.

## Why do tourist SIM cards deactivate after 90 days?

Foreigners arriving on a standard tourist visa or 90-day visa exemption are legally classified as temporary visitors. Under BTK telecommunications law:

- All SIM cards registered with a foreign passport are issued as **Tourist Prepaid Lines (Turist Hattı)**.
- These lines have a strict legal lifespan of **90 calendar days**.
- After 90 days, outgoing calls and mobile data are restricted, and the number is permanently terminated within 30 additional days unless converted.

### How to keep your Turkish phone number active

If you apply for and receive a short-term residence permit (*İkametgah*), your temporary tax number is replaced with a 99-series **Foreigner Identification Number (Yabancı Kimlik Numarası)**.

Before your 90 days elapse:
1. Take your original plastic **İkamet card**, your **passport**, and your current SIM card to an official branch of your operator.
2. Request a **Line Status Update** (*Hat Güncelleme*) to transfer your number from a tourist line to a standard resident line (*Bireysel Hat*).
3. Once converted, your SIM card will not be cut off, and you can switch to far cheaper standard postpaid contracts (*faturalı hat*).

## Step-by-step: How to officially register your IMEI on e-Devlet

If you plan to stay in Turkey long-term and decide to pay the statutory registration fee for your high-end smartphone, follow these exact steps:

1. **Retrieve your device IMEI:** Dial `*#06#` on your phone's dialpad and note down the 15-digit number for IMEI 1 (and IMEI 2 if dual-SIM).
2. **Obtain an e-Devlet Password:** Visit any PTT (Turkish Post Office) branch with your residence permit (İkamet) or passport and pay 20 TL for your official e-Devlet password.
3. **Pay the Registration Tax:** Log into the Turkish Revenue Administration portal (*Gelir İdaresi Başkanlığı - İnteraktif Vergi Dairesi*) at [gib.gov.tr](https://ivd.gib.gov.tr) or pay directly through major Turkish banking apps (Ziraat, Garanti BBVA, İşbank) under *Cep Telefonu Harcı Ödeme*. Enter your exact 15-digit IMEI number and your passport/ID number.
4. **Complete the BTK Registration:** Log in to [turkiye.gov.tr](https://www.turkiye.gov.tr) and search for **IMEI Kaydet**. Confirm your payment details, enter your passport entry date (must be within the last 365 days), and link your device's IMEI.
5. **Restart Your Phone:** Once approved on e-Devlet, your phone is permanently registered to your identity. Restart your device to establish a persistent network link.
